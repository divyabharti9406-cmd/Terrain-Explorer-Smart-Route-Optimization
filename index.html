02. Partial Derivatives

Partial derivatives measure how elevation changes independently in the X and Y directions.

Mathematical formulas

fx = 0.16x − 1.6(x−2)e−((x−2)²+(y−1)²)/5
fy = 0.16y − 1.6(y−1)e−((x−2)²+(y−1)²)/5

fx represents the rate of elevation change along X. fy represents the rate along Y.

03. Gradient Analysis

The gradient combines both partial derivatives to describe the direction of maximum elevation increase.

∇f = (fx, fy)
|∇f| = √(fx² + fy²)

06. Route Optimization

Compare a shortest-distance route with a route that considers terrain steepness.

Route cost equation

Cost = Distance × (1 + λ × Average Slope)

λ controls how strongly steepness affects route cost. This is a simplified slope-aware cost model.

Understanding the result

The shortest-distance route minimizes total travel distance. The slope-aware route minimizes the defined distance-and-steepness cost. It may travel farther to avoid steep terrain.

The route is optimal for this simulated grid and cost function, not a guarantee of real-world safety.
